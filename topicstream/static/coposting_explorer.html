Co-posting explorer

Finds bursts: the same message, or the same link, posted by several different accounts inside a short window. Load a CSV of posts, say which columns are which, and move the sliders. The file is read by this page in your browser. Nothing is uploaded and the page works with no internet connection.

How it works, and what it does not tell you

Every post gets a key: its text with hashtags stripped, its exact text, or its link. Posts with the same key are sorted by time. Starting from each post in turn, the page collects every post with that key inside the window. If those posts come from at least the minimum number of different accounts, that is a burst, every account in it is a candidate, and the search continues after the burst. "Any link containing" is a different kind of rule: no window, just every account that ever shared a link with that string in it, which is how you follow a tracking token.

A burst is a lead. People who see a post and paste it land in bursts, and a member who always posts an hour late never does. Treat the candidate list as a net: decide each account on what the account is (age, history, audience, what else it posts), and keep the people who merely agreed out of your findings. A group that posts on a published schedule is coordinated and may be entirely genuine.

When you have a window and a minimum that suit your data, the same two numbers go into TopicStream's config as burst.window_minutes and burst.minimum. From a copy of the repository, python -m topicstream explorer opens it. It is one file with no dependencies, so you can copy it to an offline machine by itself, and you may change it freely.

Times are read as UTC unless the value carries its own offset. Accepted: 2026-02-03 02:47:12, ISO 8601 (2026-02-03T02:47:12Z), and Unix seconds or milliseconds.
