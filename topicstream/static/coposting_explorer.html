Co-posting explorer

Finds bursts: the same message, or the same link, posted by several different accounts inside a short window. Load a CSV of posts, say which columns are which, and move the sliders. The file is read by this page in your browser. Nothing is uploaded and the page works with no internet connection. Reposts and replies are left out to begin with, because a repost is the same text by design; tick them back in to look at who amplified what.

How it works, and what it does not tell you

Every post gets a key, chosen in the Find accounts that posted menu: its text with hashtags stripped, its exact text, or its link. Posts with the same key are sorted by time. Starting from each post in turn, the page collects every post with that key inside the window. If those posts come from at least the minimum number of different accounts, that is a burst, every account in it is a candidate, and the search continues after the burst. Any link containing is a different kind of rule: no window, just every account that ever shared a link with that string in it, which is how you follow a tracking token.

Reposts need care. A repost carries the same text as the post it repeats, so with reposts ticked in, every popular post becomes a burst, and a wave that lasts an hour shows up as several bursts back to back; widen the window to see it as one. That view tells you which posts were amplified and by how many accounts. It says nothing about coordination by itself: a thousand strangers reposting something they like look exactly the same.

A burst is a lead. People who see a post and paste it land in bursts, and a member who always posts an hour late never does. Treat the candidate list as a net: decide each account on what the account is (age, history, audience, what else it posts), and keep the people who merely agreed out of your findings. A group that posts on a published schedule is coordinated and may be entirely genuine.

When you have a window and a minimum that suit your data, the same two numbers go into TopicStream's config as burst.window_minutes and burst.minimum. This page is part of TopicStream, which is free and open source under the MIT licence: https://github.com/ArgeliusLabs/TopicStream. From a copy of the repository, python -m topicstream explorer opens it. It is one file with no dependencies, so you can copy it to an offline machine by itself, and you may change it freely. To look at posts TopicStream collected, write them out with python -m topicstream export-csv posts.csv --config config.json and load that file here, or press Explore bursts on a case in the workspace, which opens this page on that case and is the only time the page asks a server for anything (your own machine).

Times are read as UTC unless the value carries its own offset. Accepted: 2026-02-03 02:47:12, ISO 8601 (2026-02-03T02:47:12Z), and Unix seconds or milliseconds.
